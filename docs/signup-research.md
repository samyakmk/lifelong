# Function Health signup research

## Observed on November 23, 2026

- On desktop (1440 × 1000) and at phone width (390 × 844), the landing hero showed the annual price and a **Start testing** link. The header repeated the CTA. On phone, the page tucked the full navigation into a menu while keeping that CTA in view. The page also repeated the annual cost in its membership section with a **Join Function** link. [Function Health landing page](https://www.functionhealth.com/)
- **Start testing** opened one signup screen. The form began with email or an Apple sign-in option, then a membership benefits card. It continued with legal name, phone and date of birth, optional SMS consent, biological sex, testing state, an optional access-code control, and two required agreement checkboxes above **Continue**. The phone layout stacked the fields. There was no visible numbered progress or purchase control on that screen. That form was the access boundary. No fields were filled, no agreements were checked, and Continue was left untouched. [Function signup](https://my.functionhealth.com/signup)
- The public Membership page showed the price before its CTA. It described creating an account, answering health questions, scheduling the first test, and reviewing results. How It Works described sharing health history before booking, and the FAQ described scheduling through the signed-in account. [Membership](https://www.functionhealth.com/function-membership), [How It Works](https://www.functionhealth.com/how-it-works), [FAQ](https://www.functionhealth.com/faq)

## Design decisions for Baseline

Function’s later signup screens, in-flow price placement, progress cues after Continue, and payment sequence remain unverified. For Baseline, the public price is visible before signup, account details start with email, and context questions follow account setup. A persistent plan summary echoes the early benefits card. The three-step progress indicator and the final demo consent and purchase confirmation are Baseline design decisions.

The cream background, forest cards, lime accent, and serif headings take direction from Trainer Plus. Baseline uses its own wordmark, sample report graphic, content, and responsive layouts. [Trainer Plus overview](https://trainer-plus-18466516212.us-central1.run.app/)
